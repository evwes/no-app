About wampo

wampo shows what's actually inside U.S. workplace retirement plans — the employer match, vesting schedule, fund lineup, fees, and design features of every 401(k) and ERISA 403(b) plan with 100 or more participants. Everything filed comes from public government filings; nothing is guessed.

Where the data comes from

  • Form 5500 filings (DOL EFAST2). Every plan on the site is built from its sponsor's own annual Form 5500 filing: participant counts, assets, contributions, and expense lines from Schedule H; service-provider compensation from Schedule C; insurance commissions from Schedule A; testing/safe-harbor status from Schedule R; master-trust links from Schedule D.
  • The audited financial statements attached to those filings. Fund lineups come from the auditor's Schedule of Assets (Schedule H line 4i); match formulas, vesting schedules, Roth and after-tax provisions, auto-enrollment, eligibility, and loan terms are extracted from the audit notes and quoted verbatim next to what we display. Scanned attachments are recovered with OCR and labeled as such.
  • A community-maintained overlay fills small gaps (fund tickers, some plan features). It never overrides filed data — where the two disagree, the filing wins.

What "not yet verified" means

If a field is blank or marked "not yet verified," the public filings genuinely don't state it — we show the gap rather than a guess. Three states appear throughout the site: as filed, affirmatively none (the filing says the plan doesn't have it), and not stated. Silence in a filing is never rendered as "no."

Known limitations — honestly

  • Timing. Form 5500s are filed 7–19 months after the plan year ends, so most plans show the prior plan year. The footer datestamp shows when data was last refreshed (weekly).
  • Small plans. Plans that file the short-form 5500-SF (allowed for some plans) carry no audited attachment by law — no fund lineup or feature notes exist publicly for them.
  • Master trusts. Some large employers invest through master trusts whose own filings disclose only asset categories, not funds. We link the trust and show what it files; where it files nothing readable, that's a real gap in the public record, and we say so.
  • Government and church plans. Governmental 457/401(a) plans and non-electing church plans are exempt from filing — they're absent by law, not by oversight.
  • No performance data. Filings don't contain fund returns, and we don't fabricate them. Expense ratios shown are estimates from public fund data and are always labeled "est."

Fee comparisons

Per-participant fee comparisons rank a plan's Schedule H administrative-expense line against every other filed plan in the same size cohort — filed numbers against filed numbers, recomputed on every data refresh. They exclude fund expense ratios, which are paid inside the investments.

Corrections

Every accuracy issue gets investigated and permanently logged. If your plan looks wrong or is missing, open a GitHub issue with the company name — filings are rechecked against the source PDF.

wampo is informational only and is not financial, legal, or tax advice. Verify plan details against your own plan documents before acting on them.
